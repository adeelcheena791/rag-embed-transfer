\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We measured how much of a paired retrieval gain reaches the answer when a RAG system swaps one open embedding model for another. On single-hop NQ, between \ev{T.lnq.R4} and \ev{T.lnq.R3} EM points followed each Hit@5 point that a dense encoder gained over MiniLM-L6, for both readers; the ratio stayed well below 1 under other answer metrics and a BM25 baseline, held on the full NQ corpus in a registered rerun (\ev{V6.T.dense.range}), and its size depended on the number of passages read (RQ1). The gain came from queries that newly retrieved a gold passage, partly offset by queries that kept or lost one, and many retrieval misses still contained the answer. On multi-hop HotpotQA, the Hit@5-based ratios were larger, but with Both@5 as the denominator they were \ev{V4.both.hp.min} to \ev{V4.both.hp.max}, and the EM gain sat in queries for which the encoder retrieved more supporting passages. Among dense encoders, bge-large gave a small EM gain over e5-base-v2 on HotpotQA and none detectable on NQ, and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B gave lower EM on HotpotQA (RQ2). Top-ranked non-gold passages lowered EM, gold position did not change it detectably, and ten passages narrowed the gaps between retrievers (RQ3). Retrieval-stage throughput differed \ev{E7.ratio.R1R6}-fold, approximate search returned nearly the same top passages, and on the full NQ corpus the four retrievers tested kept their order and their transfer ratios (RQ4).

Within this setup, retrieval gains overstated answer gains, and Hit@5 hid much of the multi-hop retrieval gain. We recommend that RAG studies report answer gains next to retrieval gains, with paired intervals, the baseline and the reading depth stated, and that they score multi-hop retrieval by complete evidence. Future work should test larger readers, other domains and the multi-hop setting on a full corpus, run the readers on approximate-search results, and judge by hand whether the answer-bearing passages behind retrieval misses support the answer.
