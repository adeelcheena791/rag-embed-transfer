\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We measured how much of a paired retrieval gain reaches the answer when a RAG system swaps one open embedding model for another. On single-hop NQ, between \ev{T.lnq.R4} and \ev{T.lnq.R3} EM points followed each Hit@5 point that a dense encoder gained over MiniLM-L6, for both readers; the ratio stayed well below 1 under other answer metrics and a BM25 baseline, and its size depended on the number of passages read (RQ1). The gain came from queries that newly retrieved a gold passage, partly offset by queries that kept or lost one. On multi-hop HotpotQA, the Hit@5-based ratios were larger, but with Both@5 as the denominator they were \ev{V4.both.hp.min} to \ev{V4.both.hp.max}, and the EM gain sat in queries for which the encoder retrieved more supporting passages. Among dense encoders, bge-large gave a small EM gain over e5-base-v2 on HotpotQA and none detectable on NQ, and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B gave lower EM on HotpotQA (RQ2). Top-ranked non-gold passages lowered EM, gold position did not change it detectably, and ten passages narrowed the gaps between retrievers (RQ3). Retrieval-stage throughput differed \ev{E7.ratio.R1R6}-fold, approximate search returned nearly the same top passages, and the four retrievers tested on the full NQ corpus kept their order (RQ4).

Within this setup, retrieval gains overstated answer gains, and Hit@5 hid much of the multi-hop retrieval gain. We recommend that RAG studies report answer gains next to retrieval gains, with paired intervals, the baseline and the reading depth stated, and that they score multi-hop retrieval by complete evidence. The main open question is whether the ratios hold on the full corpus; future work should also test larger readers and other domains, run the readers on approximate-search results, and audit retrieval misses for unlabelled answer-bearing passages.
