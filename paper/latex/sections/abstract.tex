\begin{abstract}
Embedding benchmarks rank retrievers in isolation.
Builders of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems need two other numbers: how much a retrieval gain improves the final answer, and what that gain costs.
We are not aware of work that measures retrieval-to-answer transfer ratios jointly with measured cost on free hardware.
We fix the reader (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ) and the corpus (a 300,000-passage BEIR-NQ sub-corpus) and vary only the retriever: BM25 and six open-source encoders from 23M to 596M parameters.
We record encoding time, query latency, index size and GPU memory on one free Tesla T4.
On 2,255 Natural Questions queries, Hit@5 ranges from 66.4\% to 81.6\% across the dense encoders, but exact match (EM) ranges only from 47.1\% to 51.0\%.
Relative to MiniLM-L6, the transfer ratio $\Delta\mathrm{EM}/\Delta\mathrm{Hit@5}$ lies between 0.19 and 0.25 for the other five dense encoders.
Within our setup, bge-large and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B need 3.0$\times$ and 7.5$\times$ the encoding time of e5-base-v2, retrieve significantly worse than it, and show no detectable EM gain.
For choosing an embedding model in RAG, we argue that the transfer ratio and measured cost say more than the benchmark rank.
\end{abstract}
