\subsection{RQ4: Quality--cost frontier}
\label{sec:rq4}
\figsix
Figure~\ref{fig:F6} shows the Pareto frontier.
For corpus encoding time and for per-query latency with GPU or CPU query encoding, the frontier is the same for EM, Hit@5 and nDCG@10: BM25, MiniLM-L6-v2, bge-small, bge-base and e5-base-v2.
bge-large and Qwen3-Emb-0.6B are dominated by e5-base-v2 on every measured cost axis and on every quality metric.
Their retrieval deficits against e5-base-v2 are significant; their EM differences from it (\NUM{d.em.R4.R5} and \NUM{d.em.R6.R5}\,pp) are not.
Every frontier step is significant on retrieval metrics; on EM, only the first step, from BM25 to MiniLM-L6-v2, is significant (MiniLM-L6-v2 minus BM25: \NUM{d.em.R1.R0}\,pp); the three dense EM steps are not.
bge-base stays on the frontier only because its encoding time is \NUM{enc.gap.R3R5}\% below that of e5-base-v2, within the measured run-to-run variability of GPU throughput (\NUM{tp.lo}\% to \NUM{tp.hi}\%).
\emph{Exploratory:} in a post-hoc sensitivity analysis that treats cost differences of at most \NUM{tie.pct}\% as ties, e5-base-v2 dominates bge-base, and the frontier becomes BM25, MiniLM-L6-v2, bge-small and e5-base-v2 on every cost axis and quality metric.
On index size (BM25 not measured), the frontier is bge-small and e5-base-v2 with or without the tie rule.
